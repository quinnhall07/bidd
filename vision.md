# Bidd: Vision

> **The closet for SEC Greek life.** Resell your rush wardrobe, shop your chapter's drops, and every sale counts for your chapter.

The name works two ways: **Bid Day** is the biggest clothing moment of the Greek year, and to **bid** is to want something before it's gone.

---

## The problem
- **Clothes worn once.** One Bama Rush outfit was tallied at **$19,373** for one week of recruitment. New-member dues already average **$4,750 a semester**. Those outfits end up in closets.
- **Chapter merch runs on Google Forms, Venmo and GroupMe.** Officers chase sizes and payments by hand for every Bid Day shirt, philanthropy tee and formal favor.
- **Existing resale wasn't built for campus.** Facebook Marketplace and Depop aren't campus-specific. Fizz is .edu-gated but it's a social app with moderation problems. Curtsy started as sorority dress rental at Ole Miss, then dropped the Greek focus to become a general Gen Z app. Greek chapters are unclaimed.

## The market
- UA alone has **10,000+ students in Greek life (about 35%)** and **2,500+ women** go through Panhellenic recruitment, the largest in the country.
- Across the SEC (approximate figures): Vanderbilt 43% Greek, Ole Miss 37%, Auburn 31%, Arkansas 28.5%, Georgia 25.5%, LSU 22.5%.
- Greek merch is real money: Fresh Prints grew from $1.9M to about **$40M** in revenue (2016–2022).
- Secondhand is a **$393B** global market by 2030, and Gen Z and Millennials drive **70%+** of its growth.

## The product
Everything on Bidd is a **closet**.

| Closet | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal closet** | Any verified student | Resell clothes. Like Depop, but only on your campus. |
| **Chapter closet** | Verified sororities, fraternities, clubs | A permanent storefront plus **drops** |
| **Brand closet** | Partners like White Fox | Co-branded chapter drops, without having to come to campus |

A **drop** is a pre-order window: a countdown, limited stock and an optional **access code** (members-only or a code posted publicly for hype). When the window closes, Bidd sends sizes and payments to the chapter's licensed vendor. **Bidd never prints anything**, so it never touches Greek or university trademarks.

### What makes Bidd different
1. **Built around chapters.** Chapter closets, drops and the **Closet Cup**.
2. **Campus-verified.** Sign-in requires a .edu email, and you belong to a verified chapter.
3. **Campus handoff.** A QR-confirmed exchange at a campus Exchange Spot, or shipping to the buyer's Mail Stop Code locker.
4. **Marketplace only.** No feed posts or DMs, which means nothing to moderate for harassment.

### The Closet Cup
Every sale a member makes counts toward their chapter's score automatically. Seasons follow the Greek calendar: the **Fall Cup** runs from Bid Day to Thanksgiving, the **Spring Cup** from formals to move-out. The winning chapter's philanthropy gets a share of Bidd's fees.

**Across campuses:** an SEC leaderboard pits the same national sorority's chapters against each other, for example Alabama's chapter vs Ole Miss's. These are rivalries chapters already have.

### The Greek clothing calendar
| When | Moment | On Bidd |
|---|---|---|
| Aug | Rush | **Rush Closet**: outfits sorted by round dress code |
| Mid-Aug | Bid Day | Bid Day shirt drops. The Fall Cup starts. |
| Sep–Nov | Gamedays | Gameday fits, home-game drops |
| Fall and spring | Philanthropy weeks | Event-shirt drops, with proceeds tagged to the philanthropy |
| Winter and spring | Formals | Formal-dress resale |
| May | Graduation and move-out | **Senior Clear-out**. The Spring Cup ends. |
| Summer | Off-season | Pre-orders for next year's Bid Day |

## Business model
| Stream | Fee |
|---|---|
| Peer resale | **0% seller fee.** Buyer pays **5% + $0.50**, which covers card processing. Matches Depop and Vinted. Poshmark tried seller-side fees and reversed them in about 18 days. |
| Chapter drops | **10% of gross**, taken from the chapter's margin and not added to the price. By default, 10% of the chapter's profit is tagged for its philanthropy. |
| Brand co-drops | **15–20% revenue share** |

## How it gets adopted
1. **Chapters first.** Each chapter brings 100+ members. Marketplaces usually win by securing supply first, and here the chapters are the supply.
2. **Selling is easier than on Facebook.** AI fills in the listing from a photo in about 30 seconds, and the handoff is on campus.
3. **The Closet Cup** gives every member a reason to list.
4. **Points** are earned only on completed sales, with a weekly cap. They're redeemed for fee waivers and early drop access. A Supe Store credit partnership is a stretch goal.

## Go-to-market
**Start with Alabama Greek life, then expand across the SEC, then to everyone else.** National sororities connect SEC chapters to the same organizations' chapters at smaller schools, so status flows down. Every campus is a tenant, and launching a new school means adding one config row.

## Why universities will want it
Sac State's Hornet Reuse had 4,000+ users and moved 3,000+ lbs of items in six months. It still shut down because investors pulled funding. Bidd is multi-tenant, so a university or the Supe Store can run its own instance. Campus partnerships make up the roadmap: Supe Store drop counters, Campus Mail, and the Career Closet.

## Roadmap (not in the hackathon build)
- Formal-dress rental
- Staffed Supe Store drop counter and peer-to-peer smart lockers (Harbor Lockers has a peer-to-peer use case and an API)
- Crimson Career Closet partnership: it lends interview clothes for free but no longer takes clothing donations because it's out of space. Bidd can hold pledged donations until the closet needs them.
- Move-out donation drive: UA SGA's drive doesn't list clothing among what it collects.
- Sustainability stats per chapter: estimated lbs kept out of landfills.

## Risks and our answers
| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Isn't this Curtsy?" | Curtsy left Greek life. Bidd is built around chapters, drops and campus handoff. |
| Licensing | Bidd never prints. Drops go through the chapter's licensed vendor. |
| Mail Center doesn't accept local drop-offs | QR Exchange Spots today, a USPS label to the buyer's MSC (allowed), and a Campus Mail partnership as a pilot. |
| Empty marketplace at launch | Seed it with post-rush outfits, leftover Bid Day shirts and the first chapter drop. |
| Seasonality | Product moments follow the Greek calendar, and summer is for pre-orders. |
